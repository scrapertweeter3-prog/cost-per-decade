A pair of socks can teach you the price of everything

A $20 pair of guaranteed-for-life socks and a $6 three-pack from a big box shelf look like the same purchase at the register. They are not the same purchase ten years later. At DurablePicks we ran the numbers on lifetime-guarantee socks from Darn Tough, Smartwool, and Icebreaker and set each against the cheap three-pack: replaced roughly twice a year, the budget socks cost about $120 over a decade, while one $20 pair that survives the whole ten years works out to $3 a year. That is the whole method in miniature, and it works on espresso machines, duffel bags, work pants, and paint sprayers the same way.

The method has three parts. First, price everything per decade, so a $200 item that lasts twenty years beats a $90 item that lasts five. Second, study how things fail: a work pant that blows out at the knee seam and a coffee machine with a proprietary boiler tell you more than any star rating. Third, read the warranty the way a claims adjuster would, because "lifetime guarantee" that excludes wear items is marketing, not coverage. Repairability belongs here too, since an E61 group head can be rebuilt with parts any tech stocks, and a sealed unit cannot.

None of this requires inventing test data, which is the part most review sites get wrong. The failure modes are documented by owners, the prices are public, and the arithmetic is simple enough to check yourself. Buy it for life is really a budgeting technique wearing work clothes: pay attention to the decade, not the receipt.

James Verlander, DurablePicks
